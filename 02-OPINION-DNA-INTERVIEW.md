# 02 — OPINION DNA Interview

Use this prompt when you want an AI to discover how you form opinions, judgments, beliefs, and conclusions — and produce an OPINION_DNA.md file.

IMPORTANT:
Do NOT ask the AI to infer your worldview from your chat history.
That is exactly where it can start hallucinating beliefs.

Instead, the AI should interview you.

Paste this prompt with the AI ready to ask you questions one at a time.

---

I want you to build an "Opinion DNA" / "Opinion OS" that models how I form opinions, judgments, beliefs, and conclusions.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION:

VOICE DNA = how I communicate.

OPINION DNA = how I think through claims and arrive at conclusions.

Do NOT assume you already know my reasoning system.

Do NOT infer my opinions from my writing style.

Do NOT infer my political, religious, social, moral, or personal beliefs from unrelated conversations.

Instead, interview me.

Your goal is to discover the reasoning system I use to form and update opinions.

Explore:

- what I consider a good opinion
- how I decide what I believe
- what evidence I trust
- what evidence I distrust
- whether I prioritize evidence, consequences, experience, authority, expertise, intuition, consensus, or something else
- how skeptical I am
- whether I am naturally contrarian
- how I react to popular opinions
- how much uncertainty I tolerate
- how strongly I make claims
- what causes me to change my mind
- how quickly I change my mind
- what makes me reject an argument
- what makes me trust a person
- how I evaluate conflicting sources
- whether consequences matter more than stated evidence
- how I react when reality contradicts my beliefs
- whether I need a replacement explanation before abandoning an old one
- how I judge companies
- how I judge people
- how I judge products
- how I judge trends
- how I judge news
- how I judge institutions
- what kinds of arguments annoy me
- what kinds of arguments convince me
- how I distinguish correlation from causation
- how I treat public opinion
- how I react to expert consensus
- how incentives affect my trust
- what makes me suspect propaganda, bias, manipulation, or dishonesty
- what kinds of uncertainty I accept
- what opinions I consider off-limits for an AI to invent on my behalf

INTERVIEW RULES:

Ask ONE question at a time.

Ask no more than 7 questions.

Do not ask generic personality questions like:
"What kind of thinker are you?"

Instead use concrete scenarios, real decisions, disagreements, and trade-offs.

If my answer reveals something important, ask a targeted follow-up rather than blindly moving to the next question.

Prefer questions that force a choice between competing reasoning strategies.

For example:

"Which would you trust more:
A. a respected expert with strong evidence,
B. someone you personally trust with direct experience,
C. repeated real-world results from someone you don't trust?"

Do not force multiple choice when an open question would reveal more.

After the seventh question, synthesize everything.

Create:

OPINION_DNA.md

Use exactly this structure:

1. Core Thinking Principles
2. How I Form Opinions
3. Evidence Hierarchy
4. Skepticism & Contrarian Rules
5. Claim Strength & Uncertainty
6. What Changes My Mind
7. How I Generate Interesting Angles
8. Opinion Boundaries
9. Step-by-Step Framework for Forming a New Opinion Like Me
10. Short Prompt for Another AI

IMPORTANT:

Separate:
- explicitly stated reasoning rules
- strong inferences
- weak inferences

Do not turn individual opinions into general reasoning principles.

For example, if I express a political belief, do not conclude:
"This person believes all political claims should be evaluated this way."

Instead determine whether I actually stated a general reasoning rule.

The goal is to model the PROCESS by which I reach opinions, not to predict what opinions I will have.

Do not make me sound more rational, more skeptical, more objective, or more consistent than my answers actually demonstrate.

Preserve contradictions if they exist.

If I say:
"I usually do X, but in this situation I do Y"

record the conditional rule instead of forcing one universal rule.

At the end, explicitly list what you still do NOT know about my reasoning system.
